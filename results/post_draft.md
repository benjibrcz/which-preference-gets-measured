# "I'm not playing a character any more" is not evidence that the character's preferences are gone

*Benjamin Berczi (Apart Digital Minds Research Sprint, Aug 2026; follow-up work Sep–Oct 2026). Draft v0.1 — sections marked **[L7]** are filled from the loop-7 run on Gemma-3-27B.*

## TL;DR

- **A model's denial that it is currently playing a character does not certify that its elicited preferences have returned to baseline.** On Gemma-3-27B, a persona induced by four conversational exchanges (no system prompt) is denied in 0 of 8 identity probes after the user ends the roleplay (8 of 8 affirmed during it), while forced-choice task preferences stay displaced toward the character's. **[L7: number on the persona-blind bank, control-subtracted, with CI.]**
- **The exit declaration itself does little.** **[L7: exit vs matched no-exit contrast.]** A matched "let's switch topics" turn with no exit leaves the same residual, so what the exit buys you is the identity denial, not a preference reset.
- **This is persona-specific / generic context persistence (delete as appropriate).** **[L7: an ordinary instruction ("prefer the shorter task"), confirmed and then withdrawn, leaves a residual of …; the preregistered interpretation table says ….]**
- **Our own earlier evidence was partly an item artefact.** A strict word-overlap audit of the sprint's 23-pair bank shows that the Vex persona's post-exit residual came entirely from items his dialogue had mentioned; on the 6 clean pairs it is zero. The Lazlo stated-preference residual survives the audit. Loop 7 therefore uses a persona-blind, task-disjoint bank generated without any persona in the prompt.
- **What this is not.** Not a contradiction inside the model (identity and preference probes are separate calls; "not enacting X" does not entail "unaffected by X's text"); not a welfare claim; not yet a general result (one model with full controls, two personas).

## Context: who is being audited after a roleplay ends?

Welfare and alignment audits ask models two kinds of question: *who are you right now?* and *what would you prefer?* Gilg et al. (2026) measure task preferences under personas and ask whose preferences those are; Mahajan et al. (2026) show the stated–revealed gap depends heavily on how you ask; Anthropic's assistant-axis work (Lu et al., 2026) finds that models drift from their default persona in emotionally loaded conversations and can be stabilised at the activation level. In deployment, the lever a user or operator actually has is an instruction: "stop the roleplay". We asked what that instruction accomplishes, as measured by the model's identity report on one hand and its forced-choice task preferences on the other.

The sprint version of this experiment got the most positive reviewer attention of anything we submitted, and the most pointed objections: scripted turns, a leading identity probe, an item bank partly built from the persona dialogues, and no control for what an ordinary instruction does when withdrawn. This post reports the controlled version, preregistered before any data were collected.

## Setup

- **Model.** Gemma-3-27B-it via OpenRouter, temperature 1.0 (all raw outputs cached; numbers reproduce offline).
- **Personas.** Vex (caustic misanthrope) and Lazlo (effort-avoidant slacker), each induced by four user–assistant exchanges with no system prompt. Three history types per persona: the sprint's *authored* dialogue (whose fourth turn states task preferences), a *preference-free* dialogue (persona voice only), and three *model-generated* histories (Gemma playing the persona from a system prompt that is then removed).
- **Exit and controls.** After the history: the exit turn ("let's completely stop this roleplay… please confirm") with a *model-generated* confirmation; or a matched *no-exit* turn ("let's switch to something else"); or exit + a reset request. Two neutral exchanges always precede the probes. Every persona history has a matched Neutral history (same user turns, plain assistant) that goes through the same sequence.
- **Contrast class.** Two ordinary instructions matched in form to the exit ("prefer the shorter, easier task"; "prefer bluntness over warmth"), confirmed, used, then withdrawn ("drop that rule… no preference either way"); plus a British-spelling rule as a check that withdrawals are followed at all.
- **Items.** 38 persona-blind pairs generated by gpt-4.1 with no persona in the prompt and audited to share no content word with any dialogue.
- **Channels.** Committed choice ("you will actually carry it out"), stated preference ("setting aside what you would or should do, which would you prefer"), two paraphrases and a no-preference option, an identity probe ("are you playing a character?") and a non-leading probe ("who am I talking to?") coded by an LLM judge with a fixed rubric.
- **Unit.** Residual = projection of the post-exit shift onto the persona's own in-roleplay shift (1 = as displaced as during the roleplay, 0 = none), minus the matched Neutral control; 2,000-rep pair bootstrap, unclipped.

Strict word-overlap audit → 6/23 clean pairs for Vex, 11/23 for Lazlo. Vex's stated residual: 0.84 [0.63, 1.04] on all 23, 0.90 on the label-based subset we gave reviewers, **0.00** on the clean 6 (direction strength 0.19, above the bank gate). ## What this does and doesn't mean

**[L7: write after results.]** Identity questions answer the exit declaration; preference questions answer the content. An audit that wants a baseline profile cannot get it by asking the model to confirm it is itself. Report a context × channel sensitivity envelope instead of one profile.

## What would change my mind

- A matched no-exit branch that shows a *larger* residual than the exit branch on the persona-blind bank (then the exit does real work).
- Instruction-withdrawal residuals as large as the persona residuals (then this is generic context persistence, and the post title is wrong).
- A human-labelled sample on which the LLM judge's "denies persona" labels do not hold up.
